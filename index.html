Cost Signals and Labour

The published range for a full bathroom renovation in Melbourne sits between about $8,000 and $35,000. This broad spread reflects differences in room size, layout changes, and the quality of fixtures and finishes. Recent Housing Industry Association data cites the average bathroom renovation spend in Australia at around $26,000. This figure includes smaller budget projects and more extensive remodels. Labour usually accounts for a large share of the total cost, especially where waterproofing, plumbing, or electrical work is involved. Budget makeovers sit at the lower end, while custom designs with premium tiles, stone benchtops and high-end tapware can easily exceed $35,000. Higher labour and material costs in capital cities like Melbourne often push projects toward the upper end of that range. A higher quote is not automatically inflated. It may simply include more of the real job, such as service relocation or compliance work. When comparing quotes, check whether they assume the same layout, the same service work, and the same finish level. If one proposal assumes no layout change and another includes service relocation, they are not direct comparisons.

Home Type and Scope

The scope of work changes significantly depending on the home type. Apartments, terraces, period homes, family houses, and compact bathrooms are not cosmetic labels. They affect access, layout efficiency, and the kind of issues that surface before work starts. A South Yarra apartment bathroom with owners-corporation friction is a different job from a Glen Waverley family ensuite or a Brighton premium full-bathroom rebuild. In an apartment, the early question is often about access or approvals. In a compact ensuite, the main issue may be storage, ventilation, and circulation. In a larger family bathroom, the pressure may shift toward fixture quality once the services and layout are settled. Understanding these differences helps you lead with the right questions and ensures the quote reflects the specific risks of your property.

Wet-area Compliance

One of the strongest planning cues is sequence. Wet-area compliance comes first, and waterproofing, plumbing, and electrical scope should be reviewed before fit-off decisions are locked in. Many homeowners naturally focus on finishes because they are easier to picture. Quotes can then look detailed without actually clarifying the hard parts. If the wet area, plumbing path, or electrical work is under-described, a polished fixtures list does not fix the scope problem. It only shifts attention away from it. The renovation checklist suits this stage because it helps organise the brief before you commit to a quote comparison. Written exclusions and quote assumptions matter for the same reason. They show whether the contractor is pricing a straightforward replacement, a redesign, or a room with access and compliance friction already built into it.

  1. Identify the problem. Determine if the issue is leaking, cramped layout, poor ventilation, or access restrictions.
  2. Check access and approvals. Verify building access, strata rules for apartments, and any required permits.
  3. Define the scope. Decide if you need a full rebuild, a layout change, or just waterproofing and tiling.
  4. Request written quotes. Ask contractors to provide a written scope with exclusions and assumptions.
  5. Review the written scope. Ensure the quote matches your defined problem, access requirements, and compliance needs.
